Why X Words Matter in Scrabble

X scores 8 points in Scrabble — the third highest tile value after Q and Z. Like Q and Z, there is only one X tile in the bag, and it rewards precise placement on premium squares.

The key to playing X well is the two-letter X-words: OX, AX, EX, XI (all valid in TWL), and XU (SOWPODS). These short words let X score in two directions simultaneously — one of the highest-efficiency plays in the game.

Top Tip

OX and AX are the most useful X plays. Placed correctly, one X tile touching two board words can score 30+ points from a single letter.

How to Play the X Tile

  1. Double-direction plays: Place X so it forms a valid word both across and down. OX, AX, and EX enable this from almost any position.
  2. Triple Letter targeting: X on a Triple Letter Square scores 24 points from X alone — more than most full words.
  3. XI and XU: XI is valid in TWL (scoring 9 base points). XU is valid in Collins SOWPODS. Both allow flexible parallel plays.
  4. Extended plays: AX becomes AXED, AXLE, AXES. EX becomes EXAM, EXCEL, EXACT. These extensions turn a 2-letter setup into a 4-5 letter play.

X Tile Mechanics: Double-Direction Scoring

The X tile (8 pts) scores its highest value through two-direction plays: placing X where it forms part of a horizontal word and a vertical cross-word simultaneously. The short X words XI (11 pts) and XU (10 pts, a monetary unit of Vietnam) are ideal for this. Place XI horizontally while the I creates a valid vertical cross-word, and you score both XI and the vertical word in one turn. On a Double Letter Score square for X, XI becomes 19 points for the horizontal word alone, plus whatever the vertical cross-word scores.

Longer X words are valuable for board lanes where a 3+ letter play is needed. EXAM, EXIST, EXACT, OXEN, AXLE, FLUX, LUXE — these cover common board positions. Study the 4-letter X words first (EXAM = 13 pts, AXLE = 11 pts, OXEN = 11 pts) before the 5-letter ones, because 4-letter X plays fit more board positions and score more per tile than longer X words where the X's value is diluted.
